Fiscal OSINT Japan

An open-source investigative project tracking Japan's public capital flows through publicly available government documents. We cross-reference primary sources from both Japanese and US institutions to reveal the structural pathways of taxpayer money.

What is Fiscal OSINT Japan

Fiscal OSINT Japan is an independent investigative project documenting how Japan's public funds flow through Special Accounts (特別会計), procurement contracts, and inter-governmental transfers — particularly those directed toward the United States.

We exclusively use publicly available primary sources: budget documents from the Ministry of Finance (MOF), audit reports from the Board of Audit of Japan (BAJ), Cabinet decisions, parliamentary records, and US government documents (White House, Defense Security Cooperation Agency, Department of the Navy budget books). No FOIA-equivalent requests, no leaked materials — every claim in our articles can be verified by any reader following the source URLs we provide.

The project is operated by SN0W8ALL LLC, an independent research and development company based in Japan. Articles are published bilingually under CC BY 4.0, datasets under CC0 1.0.

Who this project is for

  • Investigative journalists covering Japan-US relations, defense spending, or fiscal policy
  • Academic researchers in international relations, public finance, or Japanese politics
  • Policy analysts at think tanks and government agencies
  • Civic technologists interested in open data and government accountability
  • Anyone who wants to understand where Japan's public money actually goes

Editorial Policy

📋 Core Principles

  • Public sources only. No information obtained via disclosure requests or unauthorized channels.
  • Facts and inferences strictly separated. All inferences are marked with ⚠️ Inference.
  • Immediate correction of errors. Mistakes discovered after publication are corrected and logged in the article's revision history.
  • Balanced presentation. Where Japanese and US official statements differ, both are quoted in parallel.
  • Original-language citations. Quotes are presented in the original language with translations clearly marked.
  • Direct-jump source URLs. Where possible, we use Text Fragments URLs (#:~:text=...) so readers can jump directly to the quoted passage in the original document.

This methodology reflects the OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence) tradition: any claim should be verifiable by an independent reader using only public sources. If our cited URL doesn't support our claim, we want to know — corrections are welcome.

AI Methodology

This project openly discloses its use of AI tools. We believe transparency about methodology — including AI use — is part of responsible research practice.

🤖 How We Use AI

  • Articles are written collaboratively by AI (primarily Claude) and a human editor.
  • Fact-checking uses multiple AI systems (Claude / GPT / DeepSeek / Gemini) cross-referencing each other.
  • When an AI identifies an error in our previous draft, the correction is logged in the article's revision history with attribution (e.g., "DeepSeek analysis pointed out...").
  • AI is used for data aggregation, language translation assistance, and structural editing — never for fabricating sources or numbers.
  • Final editorial judgment and responsibility rest with the human editor.

We don't hide our AI use. We document it as part of the methodology. Readers can evaluate whether this approach meets their standards of evidence.

Dashboard Overview

The Japanese-language dashboard at nana6fu.github.io/fiscal-osint-japan is the project's main analytical interface. It tracks contract-level procurement data, Special Account flows, and political funding correlations across multiple Japanese government agencies.

The dashboard tracks contract-level procurement data from FY2025 (令和7年度) across six ministries, with detailed breakdowns by Special Account, contract type, single-source ratio, and post-retirement bureaucrat (amakudari) patterns. The dashboard interface is in Japanese only. Latest datasets are available in machine-readable CSV format.

What the dashboard tracks

  • Special Account flows — 19 Special Accounts across 6 ministries totaling ¥433.6 trillion in FY2025
  • Procurement contracts — FY2025: 1,886 contracts worth ¥450.4 billion (¥1M+ contracts only)
  • Regional Defense Bureau contracts — 1,133 contracts across 5 bureaus (Tohoku, Kanto, Kinki-Chubu, Kyushu, Okinawa) totaling ¥536.2 billion in FY2025
  • Single-source contract patterns — Including 100% award-rate competitive bids (suspected pre-arranged tenders)
  • Amakudari networks — Tracking former senior officials' post-retirement positions in government contractors
  • Political donation cross-referencing — Connecting contractor payments to political fund records
  • US-bound capital flows — JBIC, NEXI, and FMS payments routed through Special Accounts

Most dashboard content is updated weekly or monthly. The dashboard interface remains Japanese-only to preserve precision in Japanese administrative terminology. For machine-readable access, see the public datasets section below.

ARTICLE #1 · 2026/05/22

Tracing the ¥80 Trillion US Investment Pledge Through Japan's FILP Special Account

The July 2025 Japan-US trade agreement announced a $550 billion (~¥80 trillion) investment pledge. We trace its actual fiscal pathway: JBIC capital injections from Japan's FILP Special Account expanded 4.2× from FY2023 to FY2026, with the Ministry of Finance itself labeling the FY2025 supplementary budget as "responding to US tariffs." We document discrepancies between Japanese and US official statements through 14 primary sources.

ARTICLE #2 · 2026/05/23

Japan's One-Time Defense Funding Scheme: The Special Law That Extracted ¥1.84 Trillion from Special Accounts, and the Tomahawks Bought at Twice the US Navy Price

A new law (the Defense Funding Securing Act, 2023) overrode Japan's standard Special Account rules to transfer ¥1.84 trillion from the Foreign Exchange Fund Special Account and FILP Special Account into general account defense spending. We trace the timeline from the May 2022 Japan-US summit through to the Tomahawk LOA signing in January 2024, document the FMS payment structure that routes Japanese taxpayer funds through US Treasury trust funds, and compare Tomahawk unit prices — Japan pays approximately 2× the US Navy's domestic price.
