SPECIAL REPORT / DOMESTIC PUBLIC PROJECTS

Reconstruction Agency ¥486.4B — Only ¥85.6B (17.6%) Under the "Command Center"'s Direct Control

The remaining ¥400.8B is executed by other ministries under the "Reconstruction Agency budget" label. The structure revealed by 495 procurement contracts and 728 subsidies from R5/R6/R7.

Fiscal OSINT Japan / June 7, 2026

Note on currency: ¥1B = approximately USD 6.5 million at June 2026 exchange rates. ¥1T (trillion) = approximately USD 6.5 billion. Japanese fiscal year notation: R5 = FY2023 (April 2023–March 2024), R6 = FY2024, R7 = FY2025.

At the end of March 2026, the supply period for emergency temporary housing in Okuma and Futaba, Fukushima Prefecture was extended. Fifteen years after the Great East Japan Earthquake of March 11, 2011, people still live in temporary housing. In the same fiscal year, the FY2025 Reconstruction Agency budget allocated ¥400 million for the "extension of supply period and dismantling/removal" of these temporary housing units.

In the same Reconstruction Agency budget, a single contract for the site preparation of the Fukushima Institute for Research, Education and Innovation (F-REI) — a research institute newly established in Namie, Fukushima Prefecture — was allocated ¥10.354 billion. That is 26 times the temporary housing budget.

This report verifies the structure of money flowing under the name of "reconstruction from the Great East Japan Earthquake," based on 495 procurement contracts and 728 subsidies for FY2023 (R5) through FY2025 (R7), all published by the Reconstruction Agency itself.

The conclusion can be stated up front. Of the Reconstruction Agency's FY2025 budget of ¥486.4 billion, the Agency itself can execute only ¥85.6 billion (17.6%). The remaining ¥400.8 billion (82.4%) is executed by other ministries under the "Reconstruction Agency budget" label. The "command center" cannot directly control over 80% of its own budget.

And this structure was, in effect, chosen at the stage of the parliamentary amendment to the Reconstruction Agency Establishment Act in December 2011.

01The ¥486.4B Reconstruction Agency Budget at a Glance

The total budget for Great East Japan Earthquake reconstruction in FY2025 is ¥646.2 billion. Of this, ¥486.4 billion (75.3%) falls under the Reconstruction Agency's jurisdiction. The remaining is split among other ministries (¥159.8B), special earthquake reconstruction local allocation tax (¥65.9B), reserve funds for reconstruction acceleration and Fukushima rehabilitation (¥67.0B), and transfers to the national debt consolidation fund (¥27.0B).

The ¥486.4 billion under the Reconstruction Agency is structured as follows.

Notably, nuclear-disaster-related items account for 69% (¥335.5B). This shows that money flowing toward the post-disposal of the Fukushima Daiichi accident — decontamination, waste processing, specified return zone development, and F-REI-related projects — forms the core of the Reconstruction Agency budget.

More than two-thirds of a budget labeled as "reconstruction from the Great East Japan Earthquake" is actually allocated not to earthquake and tsunami damage but to the post-aftermath of the nuclear accident.

02The ¥32.9T Reconstruction Financing Structure

To fund recovery from the Great East Japan Earthquake, the government committed to securing a total of ¥32.9 trillion over 15 years (2011–2025). The composition of this funding is essential to understanding the structure of reconstruction.

According to the Reconstruction Agency's official "Ten-Year Review of Reconstruction Policy," the reconstruction financing framework was expanded in stages.

DateFrameworkScale
July 2011¥19T frameworkIntensive 5-year reconstruction period
January 2013¥25T frameworkUpward revision
January 2015¥26.3T frameworkUpward revision
June 2015¥32T frameworkExtended to 10-year framework
July 2020¥32.9T frameworkExtended to 15-year framework

The initial "¥19 trillion" was an estimate based on Great Hanshin-Awaji Earthquake damage figures. The Reconstruction Agency's own documents describe the early estimation as follows:

"For rescue and emergency restoration costs, projections were built up by accumulation. For other reconstruction costs, however, estimates were calculated by extrapolation, taking into account the costs of restoration and reconstruction from the Great Hanshin-Awaji Earthquake while adjusting for differences in the scale of total damage."

Source: Reconstruction Agency, "Ten-Year Review of Reconstruction Policy" Ch.2 §4

In other words, the initial figures were not grounded in actual estimates of need, and the framework was subsequently revised upward to track actual spending.

The ¥11.2T Reconstruction Tax Breakdown

Of the ¥32.9T total reconstruction financing, approximately ¥11.2T (¥10.5T for reconstruction expenditures) came from tax increases. The breakdown:

  • Special Reconstruction Income Tax (2013–2037, 25 years, 2.1% surcharge on income tax): approx. ¥9.7T (combined cap with corporate tax)
  • Special Reconstruction Corporate Tax (2012–2014, 3 years, 10% surcharge): abolished one year early in 2014
  • Per-capita resident tax surcharge (2014–2023, 10 years, ¥1,000/year): approx. ¥0.6T
  • Revenue from review of employment income deductions etc.: approx. ¥0.7T

The remaining ¥22.4T (¥32.9T − ¥10.5T) was secured through reconstruction bonds (to be repaid with Japan Post stock sales of approx. ¥4T etc.), general account transfers of ¥2.4T, non-tax revenue, spending cuts, and national asset sales.

That is: only 31.9% of total reconstruction financing came from new taxes. The majority is reconstruction bonds and general account transfers. The original principle articulated in the July 2011 "Basic Policy for Reconstruction from the Great East Japan Earthquake" — that "the burden shall not be deferred to future generations, and the present generation shall bear it together in solidarity" — does not match the structural reality that emerged.

03The "Command Center" Reality: Only 17.6% Under Direct Control

The most striking feature of the FY2025 Reconstruction Agency budget of ¥486.4B is that the Agency itself can execute only ¥85.6B (17.6%) of it.

CategoryAmountWho executes?
Reconstruction Agency direct execution¥85.6BReconstruction Agency itself contracts and disburses
Other-ministry execution (under Reconstruction Agency name)¥400.8BMLIT, MAFF, METI, MHLW, MOE, MEXT, etc.

"Reconstruction Agency jurisdiction ¥486.4B" is a formal label. In substance, the budget is recorded under the Reconstruction Agency's name while execution is divided among other ministries.

The Reconstruction Agency's official "Ten-Year Review" explains this structure as follows:

"The Great East Japan Earthquake Reconstruction Special Account is jointly administered by all ministries. Among the expenses related to reconstruction from the Great East Japan Earthquake, those related to recovery of disaster-affected areas are, pursuant to Article 4, Paragraph 2 of the Reconstruction Agency Establishment Act (Act No. 125 of 2011), each ministry's budget aggregated as the Reconstruction Agency's budget."

Source: Reconstruction Agency, "Ten-Year Review" Ch.2 §4

As a result, a large volume of budget does not appear in the Reconstruction Agency's procurement records. Among the major FY2025 line items recorded under the Reconstruction Agency name but executed by other ministries:

  • Interim storage facility projects: ¥104.5B (executed by Ministry of the Environment)
  • Specified return zone development: ¥62.0B (Ministry of the Environment)
  • Fukushima Reconstruction Acceleration Grant: ¥59.9B (Agency transfers to local governments)
  • Radioactive waste disposal: ¥41.3B (Ministry of the Environment)
  • Comprehensive Infrastructure Maintenance Grant: ¥26.0B (MLIT)
  • Rent reduction/low-income housing assistance: ¥23.0B (via local governments)
  • Specified reconstruction base development: ¥19.9B (Ministry of the Environment)
  • F-REI related projects: ¥16.4B (operating grants)

These massive figures do not appear in Reconstruction Agency procurement records. The full picture only becomes visible when the procurement records of each ministry are separately obtained. This report verifies only the ¥85.6B (17.6%) directly executed by the Reconstruction Agency.

04December 2011: What Was Decided by the Parliamentary Amendment

The current form of the Reconstruction Agency's authority was shaped during Diet deliberations in December 2011. The government's original bill assigned only limited operational authority to the Agency.

In the Cabinet-approved version of November 1, 2011, the Reconstruction Agency's operational duties were "limited to the Reconstruction Special Zone system, Reconstruction Grants, and related matters." Multiple lawmakers in the House of Representatives Special Committee on Reconstruction criticized this.

"The Reconstruction Agency under the government bill is barely different from the existing Reconstruction Headquarters. This is no different from the direction of the original bill submitted at the time of the Basic Reconstruction Act debate."

— Katsunobu Kato (LDP), Plenary Session, November 24, 2011

"Under this bill, the Reconstruction Agency's jurisdiction covers (omitted) promoting the implementation of reconstruction measures or matters related to reconstruction special zones. Fundamentally, each ministry remains the principal agent for implementing reconstruction projects, and the Agency's implementing role is limited."

— Kazuyoshi Morimoto (DPJ), Special Committee, December 2, 2011

"One-stop response in the disaster-affected areas is essential. I therefore believe that the authority over budget requests and inter-ministerial allocations should be placed within the Reconstruction Agency."

— Yasunori Saito (DPJ), Special Committee, December 2, 2011

Based on these exchanges, while Diet deliberations were ongoing, three-party amendment talks proceeded among the DPJ, LDP, and Komeito. On December 6, 2011, a five-faction joint amendment (DPJ-Independent Club, LDP-Independent Caucus, Komeito, People's New Party-New Party Japan, Sunrise Party of Japan) was submitted.

The reason for the amendment was stated by LDP lawmaker Koichi Tani:

"This amendment proceeds from a shared understanding that, in order to advance reconstruction from the Great East Japan Earthquake more smoothly and rapidly, the Reconstruction Agency's authority must be strengthened so that it can respond to the needs of the affected areas in a one-stop manner. Reflecting the questions and points raised by both ruling and opposition parties in plenary and committee sessions, and based on sincere ruling-opposition consultation, the following amendments are proposed to the Reconstruction Agency Establishment Bill."

— Koichi Tani (LDP), December 6, 2011 (amendment submission)
Source: 179th Diet, House of Representatives, Special Committee on Reconstruction, Session 13, statement 002 (National Diet Library)

Three points were added to the Reconstruction Agency's operational duties by the amendment:

  1. Coordinating and supervising the projects of administrative agencies regarding reconstruction
  2. Centralized receipt of requests from local governments regarding reconstruction projects, formulation of response policies, and improvement or promotion of projects based on those policies
  3. Submitting consolidated budget requests for reconstruction projects, formulating implementation plans, and allocating budgets to relevant administrative agencies

The third item became the legal foundation for the current structure in which "¥400.8B of the ¥486.4B Reconstruction Agency budget is executed by other ministries."

The Rejected Your Party Counter-Proposal

At the same committee, another amendment was under discussion: the counter-proposal from Your Party (Minna no Tō). According to the Reconstruction Agency's own records:

  • Place the Reconstruction Agency in Sendai (rather than Tokyo)
  • Promote administrative reform
  • Quarterly reports to the Diet
  • Advance the termination date to March 31, 2015 (i.e., a significantly shorter lifespan than the government bill)
  • Examine the transfer of authority to local governments in disaster-affected areas

This was an alternative directed away from centralization — toward devolution to disaster-affected local governments. It was rejected.

Note: Your Party's counter-proposal details are drawn from the Reconstruction Agency's official "Ten-Year Review of Reconstruction Policy" Ch.2 §2 Organization, footnote 35. The submission of Your Party's amendment is recorded in the same committee's minutes (December 6, 2011, Session 13 and Session 14).

That is, two directions of choice existed at the time.

The three parties chose a structure in which the Reconstruction Agency makes "consolidated budget requests" while actual execution remains with other ministries.

This made the Reconstruction Agency the role of a consolidated budget bookkeeper, while substantive operations remained distributed among the ministries.

Fourteen years later, the fact that ¥400.8B of the ¥486.4B FY2025 Reconstruction Agency budget (82.4%) is executed by other ministries is the consequence of that 2011 parliamentary amendment.

The fact that an option for devolution to local governments was on the table in the Diet shows that the current structure was deliberately chosen from among multiple alternatives. Why the three parties (DPJ, LDP, Komeito) chose the inter-ministerial budget distribution structure remains a question to be further verified through the period's minutes and the records of the three-party consultations. This report confines itself to structural analysis and leaves the interpretation of motive to the reader.

05The Personnel Structure: Rotating Secondments from Other Ministries

The Reconstruction Agency's vice-ministerial appointments reveal another structural feature. The most recent three Administrative Vice-Ministers and their home ministries:

No.TermNameHome ministry

Across all 14 Administrative Vice-Ministers since the Reconstruction Agency's founding, the pattern is consistent: MOF, MLIT, and MIAC rotate the position among themselves. This means the Reconstruction Agency is not a ministry that develops its own career staff but rather a destination for rotating secondments from other ministries.

Under this personnel structure, the Reconstruction Agency's decision-making cannot be fully independent of relationships with home ministries. Combined with the fact that 82.4% of the Reconstruction Agency's nominal budget is executed by other ministries, the personnel structure completes a system in which the "command center" name covers what is, in practice, a system of inter-ministerial budget sharing.

The Reconstruction Agency's directly-executed procurement reveals another feature. From FY2023 (R5) through FY2025 (R7), the Reconstruction Agency directly issued 495 procurement contracts totaling ¥28.96 billion. Of these, competitive bidding accounted for only 19.4% of contracts and 6.0% of value. The remaining majority were no-bid (discretionary) contracts.

Of these, direct compensation to individual disaster victims (¥1.35B) represents national land buyouts in zones where evacuation orders were lifted — substantively a legitimate form of victim support. Among the remaining ¥27.23B, two structural concentrations stand out:

The following chapters examine these two concentrations in detail.

07F-REI Concentration: ¥13.8B to a New Research Institute in Three Years

The Fukushima Institute for Research, Education and Innovation (F-REI) was established on April 1, 2023 in Namie, Fukushima Prefecture as a special status institute. Promoted as the "central hub of creative reconstruction," it covers research in five fields: robotics, agriculture/forestry/fisheries, energy, radiation science / drug discovery / radiation industrial use, and nuclear-disaster data. Its legal basis is the May 2022 revision of the Fukushima Reconstruction and Regeneration Special Measures Act.

In just three years since its establishment, Reconstruction Agency budget flowing to F-REI-related projects has reached ¥13.815 billion:

Notably, the core F-REI-related work (site preparation) has been awarded for three consecutive years to the Urban Renaissance Agency (UR), Tohoku Earthquake Reconstruction Support Headquarters, via no-bid contracts.

From ¥4.49 million in R5 to ¥1.005 billion in R6 (a 224× jump) to ¥10.354 billion in R7 — a 2,306× increase over three years. The contract scale grew by more than 200× in a single year.

All three contracts cite the same no-bid justification:

"Public solicitation was conducted, but no entity other than the specified entity expressed an intent to participate. The specified entity is therefore the sole feasible executor, and competition is not feasible. (Accounting Act Article 29-3 §4)"

The same reason — "public solicitation conducted, no other applicant" — has been recorded as the identical justification for three consecutive years. This indicates that formal solicitation is being conducted but that the structure under which UR Agency alone wins has become routinized.

UR is an Independent Administrative Institution under the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT). Its successive Presidents and Directors are former MLIT administrative vice-ministers and bureau directors — a classic destination for ministry retirees (amakudari). The concentration of large F-REI site preparation contracts on this MLIT-affiliated entity for three consecutive years exemplifies a fund flow pattern: Reconstruction Agency → MLIT-affiliated independent administrative institution.

Combined with the UR Agency contracts ("public solicitation conducted, no other applicant"), the picture is: both the contracting party (Reconstruction Agency) and the receiving parties (F-REI, UR) are populated with personnel from the Reconstruction Agency and other ministries.

12Conclusion: "Reconstruction" as Label, Structure for the Third Time

Summary of facts examined in this report:

  • FY2025 Reconstruction Agency jurisdiction budget: ¥486.4 billion.
  • Of this, only ¥85.6B (17.6%) can be executed by the Reconstruction Agency itself. The remaining ¥400.8B (82.4%) is executed by other ministries under the "Reconstruction Agency" label.
  • This structure was formed by the parliamentary amendment to the Reconstruction Agency Establishment Act in December 2011. While the government bill had limited the Agency's authority to "Reconstruction Special Zones / Grants," a five-party joint amendment including DPJ, LDP, and Komeito added "consolidated budget requests, allocation to relevant administrative agencies, etc."
  • At the same committee, Your Party (Minna no Tō) submitted a counter-proposal including "transfer of authority to local governments in disaster-affected areas," but it was rejected. The option of devolution to local governments did exist among the Diet's choices at the time.
  • Reconstruction Agency Administrative Vice-Ministers are rotated among the Ministry of Finance, the MLIT, and the Ministry of Internal Affairs and Communications.
  • Of the Reconstruction Agency's directly-executed procurement (495 contracts, ¥28.96B over R5+R6+R7), the vast majority are no-bid (94.0% by value).
  • Within this, F-REI site preparation via UR Agency totals ¥11.36B over three years. UR's F-REI contract value grew 2,306× — from ¥4.49M in R5 to ¥10.354B in R7.
  • F-REI's principal executives include a President who came via Reconstruction Agency Advisor, a MEXT retiree Director, and a Reconstruction Agency retiree Executive Officer.
  • Four advertising/media firms (Dentsu PR, Dentsu East Japan, Yomiuri Shimbun, Dai Nippon Printing) received ¥2.724B over three years for "overseas communication," "reputational countermeasures," and "Osaka Expo reconstruction PR" — all no-bid.
  • In the same FY2025 budget, "extension and removal" of temporary housing for still-displaced residents is ¥0.4B. This sits alongside ¥4.1B for the National Memorial and ¥10.35B for a single F-REI site preparation contract.

This series has now verified two structures of "spending other people's money on other people":

  • Volume 1 (June 4, 2026): Yokohama International Horticultural Expo ¥95.3B — Project costs grew from ¥54B to ¥95.3B (1.76×); confirmed funding ¥41.7B versus speculative ¥53.6B.
  • Volume 2 (June 7, 2026): Reconstruction Agency ¥486.4B — A "command center" that does not control 82.4% of its own jurisdiction budget.

Common to both: under banners of "public good," "reconstruction," and "national mission," tax revenue is allocated in places distant from those who pay it (taxpayers) and those for whom it is nominally collected (disaster victims).

For working-age taxpayers paying the Special Reconstruction Income Tax (2013–2037, 25 years) and future generations bearing the reconstruction bond repayment, verifying "how my taxes are being spent and for whom" from primary sources is a basic responsibility of democratic citizenship.

This report has structured a portion of the money flow under the name of "reconstruction" from the Great East Japan Earthquake, based on primary sources published by the Reconstruction Agency itself. The remaining ¥400.8B executed by other ministries — particularly Interim Storage ¥104.5B, Specified Return Zone ¥62.0B, and Fukushima Reconstruction Acceleration Grant ¥59.9B — only becomes visible when each ministry's and municipality's procurement data is obtained separately. These are the subjects of ongoing verification.

The fact that money flows toward projects broadcasting "the image of reconstruction" overseas and at the Osaka Expo, while disaster victims continue to live in temporary housing, is recorded side by side in the Reconstruction Agency's own budget documents.
