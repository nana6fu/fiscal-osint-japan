Fiscal OSINT Japan investigative articles are built around the cross-referencing and reconstruction of primary sources published by Japan's Ministry of Finance, the Cabinet, the Diet, the Board of Audit, and US government agencies. Weapons Don't Arrive, but the Money Funds U.S. Treasury Securities — Decoding Roughly 30 Years of Japan's FMS Trust Fund Through Primary Sources

Japan's cumulative FMS purchases from 1950 to 2024 total approximately $69.6 billion (about ¥10.93 trillion at JPY 157/USD as of May 2026), per U.S. DSCA. Japan's Board of Audit has flagged unresolved settlement balances continuously since 1997 — for roughly 30 years. Prepayments are invested in U.S. Treasury securities; FY2023 forex losses alone reached ¥123.9 billion. The structure is decoded entirely through primary sources from both Japanese and U.S. governments.

FMS Trust Fund Board of Audit U.S. Treasury GAO Forex Loss

① Japan's cumulative FMS purchases (FY1950–FY2024): $69.6 billion (≈ ¥10.93 trillion), per U.S. DSCA's official Historical Sales Book.
② Japan's Board of Audit has flagged FMS unresolved-settlement issues continuously since FY1997 — for roughly 30 years. FY1997 is also the year Japan joined the Accelerated Case Closure Procedures (ACCP).
③ Prepayments are deposited at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York. Only what's needed flows into the FMS Trust Fund; the remainder is invested in U.S. Treasury securities. Interest accrues in the 0QQ Account on the U.S. side.
④ In FY2023 alone, foreign exchange losses totaled ¥123.9 billion. Future-year defense liabilities roughly doubled in 5 years. The U.S. GAO has flagged the same Trust Fund problem since 1979.

ARTICLE #2 · 2026/05/23

Japan's One-Time Defense Funding Scheme — The Special Law That Extracted ¥1.84 Trillion from Special Accounts, and the Tomahawks Bought at Twice the US Navy Price

The "amount first, funding source later" structure starting from the May 2022 Japan-US summit. A new law (Defense Funding Securing Act, 2023) extracted ¥1.84 trillion from Special Accounts, channeling 80% directly into US-made weapons via FMS. We trace the structure through primary sources from both Japanese and US governments.

Special Account Defense Budget FMS Defense Funding Securing Act Tomahawk

① In FY2023, Japan transferred a one-time ¥1.84 trillion from the FEFSA and FILP Special Accounts to the general account, authorized by the Defense Funding Securing Act (Act No. 69 of 2023).
② Article 14 of the same Act locks these funds to defense expenditure. A new "Defense Capabilities Enhancement Fund" was established in the general account — effectively a quasi-defense special account.
③ FY2023 FMS procurement was ¥1.4768 trillion — quadrupled YoY, expanded 13× over a decade. Tomahawk unit prices were 1.9–2.3× the US Navy's domestic price.
④ FMS prepayments are held in the FMS Trust Fund at the US Treasury, invested in US Treasury securities. Japan's Board of Audit has flagged uncollected balances since 1997; the most recent report cited ¥1.14 trillion uncollected for 5+ years.

Tracing the ¥80 Trillion US Investment Pledge Through Japan's FILP Special Account

JBIC capital injections from Japan's FILP Special Account expanded 4.2× from FY2023 to FY2026. Japan's Ministry of Finance itself labels its FY2025 supplementary budget as "responding to US tariffs." We trace the structural pathway through 14 primary sources from both Japanese and US governments.

$550B / ¥80 trillion FILP Special Account JBIC MOU OSINT

① The $550 billion (~¥80 trillion) US investment pledge announced in July 2025 is already in motion through a concrete fiscal pathway: loans and equity from Japan's FILP Special Account to JBIC.
② JBIC inflows expanded from FY2023 ¥1.0557 trillion to FY2026 ¥4.4177 trillion — a 4.2× increase. The Ministry of Finance itself labels Japan's FY2025 supplementary budget as "responding to US tariffs."

📋 About Fiscal OSINT Japan

Editorial Policy:

  • Public sources only (no disclosure requests, no unauthorized channels)
  • Facts and inferences strictly separated (⚠️ Inference marker)
  • AI-aggregated content marked explicitly (🤖 AI Aggregation marker)
  • Immediate correction of discovered errors, logged in each article's revision history
  • Balanced presentation (Japan and US quotes presented in parallel)
  • Original-language citations with translations explicitly marked
  • Primary source links provided wherever possible, with Text Fragments URLs (#:~:text=...) where applicable

AI Methodology:

  • Articles on this site are co-authored by AI (Claude) and a human editor
  • Fact-checking uses multiple AI systems (Claude / GPT / DeepSeek / Gemini) cross-referencing each other
  • Corrections triggered by AI analysis are clearly noted in the revision history
  • AI use is disclosed, not hidden, as part of the methodology
  • Final editorial judgment and responsibility rest with the human editor
